# ChronoDesk Code of Conduct

## Purpose

ChronoDesk welcomes people who want to improve the project constructively. This code of conduct applies to repository issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, project chat used for development, and other project-managed community spaces.

## Expected behavior

Participants are expected to:

- discuss ideas and code without attacking people;
- give specific, actionable technical feedback;
- assume ordinary mistakes can be corrected;
- respect different experience levels, cultures, communication styles, and accessibility needs;
- keep security reports private when the security policy requires it;
- avoid publishing another person's private or identifying information;
- respect maintainer decisions while remaining free to disagree constructively;
- keep project spaces relevant to ChronoDesk.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following is not acceptable in project spaces:

- harassment, threats, stalking, or intimidation;
- discriminatory insults or exclusionary conduct;
- sexual harassment or unwanted sexual attention;
- deliberate humiliation or repeated personal attacks;
- doxxing or publishing private information without permission;
- spam, scams, impersonation, or malicious links;
- knowingly submitting malware, credential theft, destructive payloads, or secret material;
- sustained disruption after a maintainer asks for the behavior to stop.

Technical disagreement, firm review comments, rejecting a change, or closing an issue is not harassment when it is focused on the work and communicated professionally.

## Reporting

For ordinary conduct concerns, contact:

- supportramsandesh@gmail.com
- sanskarin@outlook.in

For security vulnerabilities, use the process in `SECURITY.md` rather than this conduct channel.

When reporting a conduct concern, include only the information needed to understand the event. Do not circulate screenshots, logs, or personal information more widely than necessary.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may take proportionate actions such as:

1. a private or public clarification;
2. a request to stop a behavior;
3. editing or removing inappropriate project content when platform tools permit it;
4. temporarily limiting participation;
5. permanently blocking participation in project-managed spaces for severe or repeated violations.

Decisions should consider severity, repetition, impact, evidence available to maintainers, and the safety of affected participants. Retaliation against someone who reports a concern in good faith is not acceptable.

## Scope and good faith

This policy governs participation in ChronoDesk project spaces. It is not intended to police unrelated private life. Reports made in bad faith to harass another participant may themselves violate this code.
